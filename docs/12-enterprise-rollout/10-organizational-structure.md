# Organizational Structure

## Organizing Teams for AI-Assisted Development

---

## 🎯 Overview

Design organizational structures that support effective AI tool adoption, including roles, responsibilities, and governance.

**Time to Master:** 3 hours

---

## 🏢 Organizational Models

### Model 1: Centralized AI Team

**Structure:**
```
CTO
 └─ Head of AI Engineering
     ├─ AI Tools Team (builds/maintains internal tools)
     ├─ AI Enablement Team (training, support)
     └─ AI Governance Team (policies, compliance)

Engineering Teams consume AI tools
```

**Pros:**
- Consistent standards
- Centralized expertise
- Efficient resource utilization

**Cons:**
- Potential bottleneck
- Distance from product teams
- Slower iteration

**Best For:** Large enterprises, regulated industries

### Model 2: Federated AI Champions

**Structure:**
```
Each Engineering Team has:
 ├─ AI Champion (part-time role)
 └─ Team Members (all AI-enabled)

Central Community of Practice:
 └─ AI Champions from all teams
```

**Pros:**
- Embedded expertise
- Faster adoption
- Team autonomy

**Cons:**
- Inconsistent standards
- Duplicated effort
- Requires mature culture

**Best For:** Mid-size companies, agile organizations

### Model 3: Hybrid Model

**Structure:**
```
Central AI Platform Team (infrastructure, tooling)
 ├─ Platform Engineers
 └─ DevEx Engineers

Engineering Teams:
 ├─ AI Champion (part-time)
 └─ Developers (AI-enabled)

Communities of Practice:
 ├─ AI Champions Network
 ├─ Security Working Group
 └─ Architecture Working Group
```

**Pros:**
- Balance of consistency and autonomy
- Shared learning
- Scalable

**Cons:**
- More coordination needed
- Requires investment in both areas

**Best For:** Most organizations at scale

## 👥 Key Roles

### AI Platform Team

**Platform Engineer:**
- Build and maintain AI tools
- Infrastructure and integrations
- Performance optimization

**Developer Experience Engineer:**
- Developer onboarding
- Documentation
- Training materials

**AI Governance Lead:**
- Policies and standards
- Compliance monitoring
- Risk management

### Engineering Teams

**AI Champion:**
- Team advocate and expert
- Local training and support
- Feedback to platform team
- Contribution to best practices

**Engineering Manager:**
- Adoption within team
- Resource allocation
- Success metrics tracking

**Developers:**
- Use AI tools effectively
- Follow best practices
- Share knowledge
- Provide feedback

### Leadership

**Head of AI Engineering / AI Director:**
- Overall strategy
- Budget and resources
- Cross-org alignment
- Executive reporting

**CTO / VP Engineering:**
- Executive sponsorship
- Strategic goals alignment
- Investment decisions
- Organizational change

## 🔄 Governance Structure

### Decision-Making Framework

**Strategic Decisions** (CTO, AI Director)
- Tool selection and procurement
- Major architecture changes
- Budget allocation
- Organization design

**Tactical Decisions** (Platform Team, Champions)
- Feature prioritization
- Standard practices
- Training curriculum
- Integration approaches

**Operational Decisions** (Teams, Champions)
- Daily tool usage
- Workflow customization
- Local optimizations
- Team-specific practices

### Governance Bodies

**AI Steering Committee**
- Quarterly meetings
- Members: CTO, AI Director, Engineering leads
- Purpose: Strategic direction, investment decisions

**AI Champions Community**
- Monthly meetings
- Members: All AI Champions
- Purpose: Share practices, solve problems, influence roadmap

**Architecture Review Board**
- As needed
- Members: Senior architects, security leads
- Purpose: Review major AI-assisted changes
